# Executive and public-health briefs

Use this reference only when the requested healthcare interface includes an executive assessment, public-health briefing, or related communication workflow. Ordinary patient portals, scheduling, medication interfaces, and clinical records do not need a command dashboard.

This is a design synthesis for executive public-health interfaces. Primary sources below were checked September 28, 2026. Proposed patterns do not establish incident doctrine or authorize operating a live incident system.

## Preserve meaning and authority

Separate source observations, analyst assessment, recommendation, authorized decision, and completed action. Acknowledging a brief does not approve its recommendation. Do not assign threat levels, clinical/public-health thresholds, deadlines, or decision authority from a template.

For a leader-facing brief, show the supplied recommendation or decision request, affected scope, decision owner, deadline and time zone if established, important uncertainty, and next review. An evidence view can carry sources, assumptions, model/version, dissent, and history. Keep caveats that change interpretation beside the claim, not hidden in a drawer.

Treat an internal executive brief, a clinician advisory, and a public protective-action warning as different audiences with different content and approval needs. Drafting a message does not authorize sending it or activating a response.

## Risk, uncertainty, and options

Keep the outcome's estimated likelihood, possible consequence, and confidence in its evidence distinct. Use approved terms and confidence rubrics. Show an unassessed decision effect as unknown rather than writing that uncertainty cannot change the action.

Retain outcome definition, population/place, horizon, units, and the method behind quantitative claims. A fraction of deliberately selected or unweighted model scenarios is not automatically a real-world event probability. Illustrative values must be labeled where shown; omit unsupported precision.

For supplied options, preserve benefits, harms, costs, reversibility, and uncertainty against the stated objective. When a threshold matters, distinguish probability of exceedance from its magnitude and duration. The [Decision Uncertainty Toolkit](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0332522) is a methods paper with a hypothetical demonstration; it does not prove a specific interface or policy is best. Do not auto-select the option with the smallest percentage.

MONITOR / PREPARE / ACT NOW, a six-dimension risk card, and a 60-second comprehension target are proposed interface patterns, not universal government standards. Use actual agency terminology and test comprehension for the task. Missing evidence alone does not justify reassurance or a MONITOR state.

## Updates and follow-through

Distinguish a new observation, corrected/backfilled data, expanded coverage, changed assessment, changed confidence, changed recommendation, and an unchanged refresh. A corrected count is not necessarily an improvement in real-world conditions.

Separate the observation period, source publication, ingestion, assessment, and screen-refresh times where they affect interpretation. If a feed fails, preserve the true age and availability status; a fresh screen clock must not make old data look current. [CDC RESP-NET](https://www.cdc.gov/resp-net/dashboard/index.html) illustrates why recent observations and modeled estimates need explicit labels and why prior values may change.

Tie a decision to its reviewed assessment version and actual authority. Revalidate material updates before commitment. Keep recorded, dispatched, received, acknowledged, and completed states distinct. Connect follow-up to the existing operations system, owner, and approved review trigger. These are interface/contract recommendations, not proof that the backend provides durable records or execution.

## Communication and evaluation

[CDC risk-communication guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/public-health-risk-communication/php/about/understanding-and-applying-risk-communication-principles.html) supports stating knowns and unknowns, explaining valid numbers, and setting update expectations. [USWDS visualization guidance](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/data-visualizations/) supports focused charts and text explanations with accessible underlying data. Neither validates a fixed screen layout.

For public warnings, use the authorized source, hazard/impact, affected area/group, approved action, relevant timing, and route to more information. [FEMA warning-content guidance](https://emilms.fema.gov/is_0242c/groups/131.html) supports completeness. Verify the actual channel's current capabilities rather than hardcoding WEA character or language limits into every composer. Preserve source-message and translation versions and the organization's approval state.

Test sequences: weak evidence, corroboration without risk escalation, a correction, conflicting reports, revised recommendation, stale data, and de-escalation under approved rules. Ask whether intended users can explain what changed, the uncertainty, their authority, and the next step. Evaluate comprehension and process separately from agreement with a policy recommendation; acceptable policy choices require domain/operations judgment. Agent checks cannot establish real leader performance or operational readiness.
