# Evidence and research review

Verified against the public sources listed below on **2026-09-28**. Recheck versions and product applicability before making a standards, legal, clinical, or regulatory claim. This is a focused review of consequential claims, not a systematic review or certification.

## Provenance and scope

The workflow guidance draws on the public primary sources below. Empirical results, official recommendations, applicable requirements, and interface design inferences are distinguished. No prior skill installation or private research document is required.

This skill covers clinician, patient, caregiver, and care-administration software. Clinical risk determines the depth of evaluation; an appointment form and an infusion control do not have identical obligations. Neither healthcare branding nor a research label settles device or privacy-law scope. Concrete layouts, status models and test scenarios are design proposals to evaluate locally unless tied to an applicable product requirement.

## Corrections and qualifications

| Claim or implication examined | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Spatial ICU EHR accuracy improved by 6.3 **percentage points** | Correct to **5.84 percentage points**: 98.70 − 92.86. The paper's approximately 6.3% is a relative increase, not a percentage-point change. |
| Spatial ICU study means better patient outcomes | Unsupported extrapolation. It measured standardized documentation in a simulation with 36 nurses. |
| Twinlist effects describe all medication-reconciliation interfaces | Unsupported generalization. Preserve study context; do not promise a percentage improvement for a new design. |
| Monitor positioning establishes the best layout everywhere | Unsupported generalization. The tested physical setup and procedural simulation bound the result. |
| FDA/IEC expectations are mandatory for every healthcare app | Incorrect scope. Device applicability and legal requirements must be established separately; guidance is not generally binding law. |
| WCAG 2.2 is a universal legal target, or WCAG 3 is ready for conformance | Incorrect. 2.2 AA is the skill's web design baseline; actual obligations vary. WCAG 3 remains an incomplete draft at review date. |
| 7±2, four chunks, Hick/Fitts, or Pareto gives a universal screen/menu quota | Not supported. Use memory, choice and motor findings as context-dependent evidence, not fixed layout formulas. |
| Alert suppression follows automatically from high override rates | Not supported. Appropriateness, missed events and consequences need clinical evaluation; a UI skill must not alter clinical rules itself. |
| A checklist, five participants, SUS score, or agent test proves safety/compliance | Not supported. Match evidence to the claim and intended use. |

## Original empirical claims recovered

The results below are reference evidence, not target metrics or clinical protocols. Abstracts/public records were checked; where full text was unavailable, no claim is made to have independently assessed raw data, every method, or study bias. Full publications should be reviewed before basing a consequential product claim on a particular effect size.

| Primary source and checked result | Limit and use in the skill |
|---|---|
| [Twinlist evaluation, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25665706/), [public article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485926/), DOI `10.1093/jamia/ocu021`. The publication reports 20 providers, 80 trials, 18% faster completion, 40% fewer clicks, 60% fewer scrolls, and 12 versus 31 serious errors. | Small interface experiment; error counts are trial outcomes, not patient-harm estimates. Supports testing explicit comparison and grouping. Do not conflate this evaluation with the separate 2013 Twinlist prototype paper. Public indexed article excerpts were available; direct full-text retrieval was intermittently blocked. |
| [Ratwani et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29982549/), DOI `10.1093/jamia/ocy088`. Four sites, two vendors, 12–15 emergency physicians per site and six scenarios. Certain tasks differed by about nine-fold in time and eight-fold in clicks. | These are task-specific differences, not average benefits from redesign. A comparative implementation study does not isolate vendor design from configuration. Supports local workflow evaluation and measuring errors alongside time. |
| [Shah et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16221941/), DOI `10.1197/jamia.M1868`. Six months across 31 practices: 18,115 alerts, 71% noninterruptive, 29% interruptive; 67% of interruptive alerts accepted. | Descriptive deployment with clinical knowledge-base selection, not a randomized test of visual tiering alone. The percentages are not recommended quotas. Supports investigating actionability and interruption burden with clinical owners. |
| [Bates et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9794308/), DOI `10.1001/jama.280.15.1311`. The before-after comparison reported a 55% reduction in nonintercepted serious medication errors, from 10.7 to 4.86 per 1,000 patient-days. | System/process intervention; not evidence that one UI component reduces errors 55%. The separate team comparison was randomized. Potential adverse-event errors and actual preventable adverse events must not be conflated; the reported 17% decline in preventable adverse events was not statistically significant. |
| [Spatial ICU EHR trial, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42486461/), DOI `10.1055/a-2903-5822`. A randomized crossover simulation with 36 ICU nurses reported 177 seconds/27% faster tasks, NASA-TLX scores 18.44 versus 37.82 (51.3% lower), and accuracy 98.70% versus 92.86%. SUS differences were not statistically significant. | Correct accuracy difference: **5.84 percentage points**, approximately **6.3% relative**. The abstract discloses an author's employment by the interface IP owner. Supports a testable hypothesis about spatial organization, not universal superiority or patient-outcome benefit. |
| [Monitor-position study, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42550697/), [publisher repository](https://repositories.cdlib.org/uc/item/6cv87835), DOI `10.5811/westjem.52933`. There were 34 subjects/68 simulated intubation attempts. Reported median recognition times were head 37 seconds, left 32, right 23; adjusted Cox comparisons favored either side. | Results concern this procedural simulation and setup. The reported failure-rate comparison was not statistically significant. Supports testing actual sight lines; does not establish a universal right-side-monitor rule or reduced patient harm. |
| [NIST GCR 15-996, 2015](https://www.nist.gov/publications/technical-basis-user-interface-design-health-it). The public report summary confirms 559 survey respondents, 86 observations/interviews, five EHRs tested with 63 users, six experts, and over 300 EHR-specific principles. | Mixed-method applied design research, not 300 independently proven universal rules. Supports combining observation, testing and expert inspection. |

## Authorities: requirements, standards and guidance

- **Device human factors:** [FDA final guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/applying-human-factors-and-usability-engineering-medical-devices), [PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/80481/download), issued **August 3, 2026**, originally February 3, 2016. It addresses intended users, uses, environments, critical tasks and use-related risk. It generally contains nonbinding recommendations except where applicable requirements are cited. The skill's clinical review format is an adaptation, not a substitute for a device human-factors process.
- **Device standard:** [IEC 62366-1:2015+AMD1:2020 catalogue](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/67220), edition 1.1. The public abstract confirms a usability-engineering process addressing medical-device safety. Only the catalogue was reviewed; the licensed standard was not. Do not invent clause requirements or claim conformity. It is IEC 62366-1, not “ISO 62366.” Alarm-related equipment requires its own applicability assessment; this skill does not specify an alarm system.
- **EHR evaluation:** [NISTIR 7804](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nistir-7804-technical-evaluation-testing-and-validation-usability-electronic-health), 2012, describes expert review and testing with representative users performing realistic EHR tasks. Its example scenarios are adaptable guidance.
- **EHR operations:** [ONC SAFER Guides](https://healthit.gov/clinical-quality-and-safety/safer-guides), current public collection includes 2025 guides. Relevant topics include identity, ordering/CDS, results, communication and resilience. Program reporting may require a specified guide version; the newest design guidance does not automatically establish that requirement.
- **Web accessibility:** [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is the normative source for criteria and exceptions. The skill uses AA as a design baseline, not a legal determination. Complete processes and applicable criteria must be evaluated before a conformance statement. WCAG 2.2 removes 4.1.1 Parsing; policies requiring older versions may retain related reporting. [W3C's WCAG 3 introduction](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/wcag3-intro/) identifies 3 as an incomplete draft and recommends meeting 2.2 now.
- **U.S. privacy:** [HHS Security Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html) describes safeguards for ePHI and covered-entity/business-associate scope. [HHS minimum necessary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html) includes exceptions such as provider disclosures or requests for treatment. Role-aware screens and secure draft/session handling are design inferences, not mandated layouts or timeout durations. Do not present a proposed rule as current law.
- **GDPR:** [EDPB lawful-processing guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en) distinguishes an Article 6 basis from additional Article 9 conditions for health/sensitive data. Consent is one possible route. Product-specific legal basis, jurisdiction, age/proxy rules and retention remain privacy-owner decisions. Clinical consent, research participation and privacy consent are not interchangeable.
- **Patient comprehension:** [AHRQ Teach-Back Tool 5](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html) supports checking whether communication was understood. Asking participants to explain their next step during interface testing is a design adaptation; it evaluates the interface, not the person's intelligence.
- **AI transparency:** [FDA/Health Canada/MHRA principles](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/transparency-machine-learning-enabled-medical-devices-guiding-principles) cover audience-appropriate information about ML-enabled medical devices. Extending source visibility, limitations and review states to other healthcare AI is a design inference; a confidence label does not establish clinical validity.

## Cognitive evidence without layout folklore

- [Cowan, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/) places capacity estimates in carefully controlled conditions and distinguishes chunking/rehearsal effects. Externalizing relevant context is a design inference, not a four-item or seven-item screen limit.
- [Hick, 1952](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470215208416600) studied choice-reaction tasks. Improving search and reducing irrelevant choices is a reasonable interface hypothesis; deleting valid clinical choices or predicting clinical decision time from a menu count is not established by that study.
- [Fitts, 1954](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0055392) studied movement amplitude and tolerance. Give controls usable targets while testing slip risk; the experiment does not prescribe clinical button placement.
- Grouping, consistency, familiarity and recognition are useful design heuristics. This skill does not treat Gestalt, Nielsen, Jakob or Pareto labels as clinical safety laws or quantitative guarantees. Their full historical literature was not systematically reviewed.

## Coverage and remaining gaps

The reviewed themes are retained in task patterns and evaluation: identity, cognitive support, clinical density, units/time/provenance, status, ordering/reconciliation, alerts, consent, accessible interaction and recovery. A universal release gate and illustrative project calendar were not adopted. Patient/caregiver comprehension and AI-review states draw on the authorities above.

No independent replication, patient-outcome trial, cross-jurisdiction legal audit, licensed IEC clause review, or complete accessibility evaluation was performed. Numerical study claims are kept here for traceability, not embedded in the skill's operational rules. Product-specific clinical logic, proxy permissions, alarm priorities, consent basis and use environments still require their appropriate owners and evidence.
